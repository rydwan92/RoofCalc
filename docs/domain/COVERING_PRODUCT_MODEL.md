## V18 conclusions

- The supported technical families are discriminated as `roof-tile`, `modular-sheet`, and `standing-seam`.
- Physical size, effective/covering size, gauge, installation mode, pitch limit, and panel-length model are distinct concepts.
- A project stores an immutable technical snapshot. An optional catalogue reference identifies where it came from; it does not replace the snapshot.
- Technical schemas contain no price, currency, supplier SKU, stock, or discount fields.
- Compatibility is a structured result. Missing mode or batten gauge is reported explicitly and never silently defaulted.
- V18 defines strategy inputs and outputs but intentionally does not calculate covering quantities. Tile, sheet, and seam layout algorithms belong to V19/V20.

## Quantity bridge

Future covering engines receive neutral roof-plane geometry, canonical openings, and the selected technical snapshot. Their results can implement a covering quantity source consumed by `quantity-core`. `quantity-core` remains the aggregation boundary; `roof-math` must not import product, catalogue, price, or commercial data.

### V19 model consequences

- A roof-tile installation mode must be able to state both layers on one batten and offsets repeated between batten rows.
- Pattern offsets use normalized cover width and are product snapshot data, never inferred from material or manufacturer.
- Physical length is not the course step. Actual resolved batten stations are the vertical source of truth, while the mode gauge remains a compatibility range.
- Minimum/recommended pitches and underlay conditions are not interchangeable. V19 stores an optional mode minimum for compatibility but does not model the full conditional roofing-system approval.
- Declared pcs/m² yields a transparent reference range from net area. The geometric layout remains the source of V19 piece-position quantities.

### V21 model consequences and limitations

- `effectiveWidthMm` and fixed `effectiveLengthMm` are the geometric coverage cell. Total width and length remain physical/reference information and must not expand the grid.
- Module length is not assumed from manufacturer identity. When neutral resolved regular battens are available, their relevant spacing is compared with the snapshot's `moduleLengthMm`; the engine never creates or repairs a batten layout.
- The fixed-sheet solver uses one eave-based V grid and one coherent U grid per plane. Generic polygon/opening clipping handles gable and hip shapes without roof-type or manufacturer branches.
- Manufacturer-specific first/eave/ridge support offsets, accessory systems, fastening, underlay conditions and installation approvals are outside the generic V21 compatibility result.
- A geometric position count is not a purchase quantity. Waste, breakage, packaging, stock, offcut reuse and disconnected-fragment fabrication remain unresolved.
- Cut-to-length products require a separate strategy for permitted panel lengths, module-step segmentation, transverse joints/overlaps, transport/manufacturing maxima, openings and multi-panel slopes. V21 reports that length model as limited instead of imitating fixed sheets.

### V22 model consequences

- Effective width controls the coherent plane-local U grid; total width remains technical reference. Width mode selection is explicit when a snapshot offers multiple modes. There is no manufacturer-name branch.
- A geometric panel candidate is one connected visible component of a nominal U strip after roof-polygon clipping and opening subtraction. A full-width opening can therefore create separate upper and lower runs. A partial-width opening may produce a notched connected component; detailing that notch and flashing is outside V22.
- Panel `lengthMm` is the longest visible V extent of the connected component, suitable for identifying a geometric maximum requirement on a hip-cut piece. It is not a manufacturing order length: end allowances and transverse details are absent. Narrowing hip planes can produce shorter outer runs; a run touching a ridge point can still reach the full slope length.
- The generic kernel reports runs outside the snapshot's minimum/maximum range; the standing-seam adapter maps an overlong run to `transverse-joint-required` without creating a joint. The published overlap is not sufficient to derive its position.
- Exact mm run lengths are grouped within a 1e-7 mm numerical tolerance, independent of display rounding. Piece count and aggregate geometric length remain separate facts. No waste, offcuts, accessories or purchase quantity is inferred.
